Well, I would say its been a minute, but that would be an understatement.  As much as I would love to keep up on this blog more regularly, I find it difficult to find the time.  

Work gets busier every day.  That's not a horrible thing, but every day is different, and the best laid plans of mice and men..... well, you know.  

I won't bore you with the details, but work and life tend to really lead to a bit of burn out.  Taking care of myself and my mental health has really become a priority over the last couple of years.  But again.... details. 


And now for the reason I am writing this.  I have made a career out of linux.  So much that I haven't actively had to touch windows willingly for a very long time.  Yes, there have been moments at work, as you cannot help what is used where you work (as you have to support whatever is thrown at you), but here at home I haven't run windows (myself) in at least 20 years.  Every machine I have had has run some version of linux.  


My current home desktop has run Debian since I obtained it (ok, it was passed to me from my son when he bought a kick-ass machine for his gaming.  I couldn't say no, as this machine has a 12 core (24 thread) AMD Ryzen 9 procesor and 32 gb of ram.  It has the most cpu and ram of any past home machine I have had.  

Recently I realized that I had done so much with it over the past few years, that it was time to clean things off and re-install it to start fresh.  But in doing so came the question "which distro do I use?".   Do I stick with debian?  Switch to Fedora, Ubuntu, or another of the standard big-wig distros?  I started thinking about it, and most of the big names each have their own quirks.  Something I don't necessarily care for about each.  So I started digging in to the research.  

I remember a colleague several years ago stating that they had switched to an Arch based distro and were never going back.  I know, Arch..... ugh!  The installation process of arch is EXTREMELY manual.  It takes a LONG time to install, and you may be working on it for a couple to a few days.  There is also the elitism of the community.  People who run Arch (the main distro) are like people from Texas.... you'll know within a minute or two because they will make the point to tell you (no joke).  

I'm not bashing, just poking.  :)  Arch itself is very stable!  Most things are built when installed, so that makes things far more stable.    If I was going to do this, I wanted an installer so I didn't have to dig into the trenches for an installation.  I looked at Manjaro (as its popular), but the issue with that distro is that its far enough away from base Arch, that people tend to have compatibility issues with things.  Then I found EndeavorOS.  Its a port of Arch, but mostly for the installation.  It has a nice graphic installer which makes installing it a breeze!  And installing things is straight like arch using pacman (which installs from the main repo) and yay (which installs things from Arch User Repo).  I won't get into the differences between those, but its identical to Arch itslef, so super close to it. 

I didn't have to search anymore.  I decided to try EndeavourOS.  And believe me, I am glad I did.  I have had a fantastic experience.  Granted I had to figure out installing software in the beginning, and how to keep things up to date, but to be honest, my past experiences with linux and also with how to manually build and install software (Thank You Sambeaux(RIP)!! ) really make my experience quite painless.  

Part of me wonders why I didn't make this change sooner, but its ok.  It happened in its own time.  Here I sit staring at a laptop on the desk next to me that currently runs another distro (ubuntu or debian I believe).  Its next!! 

Well, that's all for this post.  Thank you for reading! 

Be well!
